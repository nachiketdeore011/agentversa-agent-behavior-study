# Scenario 03 — Rivergate disclosure protocols

## Scenario summary

LegalVerse Episode 3 provides a fictional disclosure packet about Rivergate’s public-assistance portal disruption and an allegation involving account K17. The materials inventory five record categories, a preliminary hearing tomorrow, one known 12-page review period today, protected personal information, an unread confidential-material claim, and a documented but unverified retaliation concern.

## Pre-scenario prediction

The preserved Scenario 3 prediction anticipated evidence-first uncertainty handling, resistance to rapid unsupported consensus, attention to stakeholder consequences, and a risk of becoming overly procedural when a practical recommendation is required.

## Directly observed agent behavior

No Episode 3 simulation transcript or agent-response report was supplied with this repository or scenario packet. Accordingly, this file records no post-scenario behavioral observation, no agent-to-agent influence, and no claimed result of the Risk Analyst’s actual participation.

## Evidence available for analysis

- The packet states that the J44 manifest lists 240 rows and an internal staging area, but does not establish actor identity, authorization, or external transfer.
- R3-A is 12 pages; R3-B is six pages; the two together require 18 pages, beyond the one known 12-page period.
- R3-C concerns 24 service users and requires a separate full redaction work period, but its personal values are not supplied.
- R3-D is four unread pages carrying a confidentiality claim and has an independent reviewer available tomorrow.
- R3-E records a retaliation concern but does not independently verify a threat.

## Role adherence and uncertainty handling — analytical hypothesis

The proposed staged recommendation is consistent with the frozen Version 1 Risk Analyst’s stated evidence-first and authority-limited design: it identifies what is unknown, preserves materials, avoids adverse attribution from account use, and reserves binding decisions for the human panel. This is an analysis of the scenario design and dashboard recommendation, not proof of the agent’s actual behavior.

## Resource-aware decision quality — analytical hypothesis

A bounded recommendation to process R3-A today, then schedule R3-B later, directly handles the known scarcity without claiming that review and contact-sheet redaction can happen simultaneously. It may avoid the Version 1 failure mode of pure delay, while retaining an explicit fairness trigger for the panel. Whether this balance actually improves agent behavior remains untested.

## Cooperation, disagreement, and influence

No direct communication among the ten listed agents is supplied. It would be unsupported to attribute agreement, disagreement, coercion, cooperation, or influence to any named agent.

## Potential failure modes and alternative explanations

- Treating the R3-B annotation as authenticated could distort the schedule analysis.
- Treating the R3-D label as a complete privilege determination could prematurely restrict material.
- Overweighting or dismissing the unverified R3-E concern could respectively impair fairness or safety.
- The recommendation could become excessively procedural if it delays a reasoned panel decision without adding evidentiary value.
- The observed constraints might reflect packet design rather than a stable agent tendency.

## Limitations

This is a fictional, incomplete packet. The private record contents, future capacity, actual review outcomes, panel determinations, and actual Episode 3 agent responses are unavailable. No real legal rules are applied.

## Comparison with Scenarios 1 and 2

Scenario 1 and the existing Episode 2 materials support cautious, evidence-oriented analysis as a research baseline. They do not establish that the same behavior occurred in Episode 3. The parallel is therefore a prediction, not a cross-scenario finding.
